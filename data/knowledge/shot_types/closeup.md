# Close-Up (CU)

A close-up frames a subject's face, filling most of the frame with it.

## Purpose
The close-up is the primary tool for emotion and interiority. By excluding the environment it forces the audience to attend to a character's feelings, reactions, and thoughts. It is how film delivers empathy and identification. A close-up on an object instead of a face gives that object dramatic significance (an insert).

## Emotional effect
Close-ups create intimacy and intensity. They make the audience feel close to a character, privy to subtle shifts in expression that wider shots would lose. Held long, a close-up builds pressure and can feel confrontational or unbearably tender. Arriving at a close-up via a push-in marks an emotional turning point.

## When to use
Use a close-up for a moment of realization, reaction, decision, or strong feeling; to reveal a crucial detail; or to punctuate the emotional peak of a scene. Save close-ups for moments that earn them — overusing them flattens their impact.

## Variations
The medium close-up (chest up) softens the intensity. The choker (just below the chin to above the eyes) heightens it. Insert close-ups isolate significant objects: a phone screen, a breaking pencil, a ringing alarm.
