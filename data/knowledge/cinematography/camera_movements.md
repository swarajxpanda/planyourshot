# Camera Movement

Movement directs attention, reveals information, and carries emotion. Every move should have a motivation.

## Static (locked-off)
A still camera is stable and observational. It lets performance and composition carry the scene and can feel formal, patient, or tense. Holding still while action builds creates disciplined suspense.

## Push-in / dolly-in
Moving the camera slowly toward a subject intensifies emotion and draws the audience into their internal state. A slow push-in on a face during a realization is one of the most reliable tools for marking an emotional turn. A fast push-in is aggressive and shocking.

## Pull-out / dolly-out
Pulling back reveals context, isolates a character in a newly-revealed space, or releases tension. It can abandon a character, making them small and alone as the frame widens.

## Pan and tilt
Panning (horizontal pivot) and tilting (vertical pivot) follow action or reveal information from a fixed position. A reveal via pan or tilt controls the timing of what the audience learns.

## Tracking / dolly
The camera travels alongside, ahead of, or behind a moving subject, keeping them in a stable relationship to the frame. Tracking creates momentum and immersion.

## Handheld
Handheld introduces instability and human presence. It reads as documentary realism, chaos, urgency, or subjective agitation. The degree of shake is an emotional dial.

## Crane / vertical moves
Rising reveals scale and grandeur or offers a god's-eye detachment; descending closes in on the human and the specific.
