# Composition

Composition is the arrangement of visual elements to guide the eye and encode meaning.

## Rule of thirds
Placing key elements along thirds lines or at their intersections creates a balanced, dynamic frame. It is a reliable default that leaves room for movement and eyelines.

## Leading lines
Lines in the environment — roads, corridors, edges, light beams — draw the eye toward the subject or into the depth of the frame. They can point at what matters or create a sense of inevitability.

## Symmetry and centering
Symmetrical, centered framing feels formal, controlled, unnatural, or confrontational. It can suggest order, authority, or a character's rigidity, and is powerful when broken.

## Depth and layering
Staging elements in foreground, midground, and background creates depth and lets multiple planes of action coexist. Foreground objects can frame or partially obscure the subject, adding intimacy or the sense of watching.

## Eyelines and the line of action
Where a character looks guides the audience's attention and implies offscreen space. Consistent screen direction (the 180-degree rule) keeps spatial relationships legible; deliberately crossing the line disorients.

## Balance of visual weight
Bright areas, faces, sharp focus, and high contrast attract the eye. Distributing or concentrating this weight controls where the viewer looks and how stable the frame feels.
