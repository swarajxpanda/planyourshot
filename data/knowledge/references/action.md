# Shooting for Action

Action sequences are built on clarity, energy, and physical stakes. The paradox is that chaos on screen requires precision behind the camera.

## Core principles
The audience must understand the geography — who is where, moving in which direction, toward what goal. Energy without clarity is noise. Establish space before fragmenting it.

## Framing and composition
Establish the geography with a wide shot, then move closer for impact. Strong diagonals and dynamic, unbalanced compositions create energy. Consistent screen direction keeps a chase or fight legible; crossing the line disorients (sometimes deliberately).

## Camera movement
Movement carries kinetic energy. Tracking shots that travel with a running or fighting subject create immersion and momentum. Handheld introduces urgency and chaos, but excessive shake destroys legibility. Whip pans and fast moves can transition or intensify.

## Lensing and focus
Wider lenses exaggerate speed and space as subjects rush toward or past the camera, and keep more in focus during fast movement. Longer lenses can compress a charge or isolate a moment of impact.

## Lighting
Action lighting favors contrast and directionality for drama, but must keep the subject readable. Motivated flashes, strobing, and shifting sources can add chaos while preserving a clear silhouette.

## Editing rhythm
Shorter shots and faster cutting accelerate perceived speed and impact. But each cut should advance clarity — the viewer should always know what just happened and what is at stake. Punctuate with a held beat to let a major hit land.
