# Shooting for Romance and Intimacy

Romantic and intimate scenes are built on closeness, warmth, and the exchange of looks.

## Core principles
Intimacy is created by reducing distance — between characters, and between the audience and the characters. The camera should feel tender and unhurried, giving space for small gestures and glances to register.

## Framing and composition
Use close-ups and medium close-ups to hold the audience near the characters' faces. Two-shots that place the couple close together, sharing the frame, show connection; the shrinking physical gap between them can be staged and reframed as intimacy grows. Soft, shallow focus isolates the couple from the world around them.

## Camera movement
Slow, smooth movement — a gentle push-in, a soft drift — mirrors emotional opening. Avoid harsh or jittery motion. Circular or floating moves around a couple can heighten a sense of the world falling away.

## Lensing and focus
Longer lenses with shallow depth of field blur the background into soft bokeh, wrapping the couple in a private space. Focus on the eyes; rack focus can pass attention tenderly between two faces.

## Lighting
Warm color temperature and soft, low-contrast light flatter skin and feel intimate and safe. Motivated practical sources — candlelight, a warm lamp, golden-hour window light — ground the warmth in the scene. Gentle backlight can create a soft halo.

## Rhythm
Let shots breathe. Longer takes and unhurried cutting give romance room to feel genuine rather than rushed.
