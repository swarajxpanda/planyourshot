# Wide Shot (WS) / Establishing Shot / Long Shot

A wide shot frames the full subject within a substantial amount of the surrounding environment.

## Purpose
The wide shot establishes geography — where we are, who is present, and how they are arranged in space. As an establishing shot it opens a scene by orienting the audience before cutting closer. It shows the relationship between characters and their environment.

## Emotional effect
Placing a character small within a large frame conveys isolation, vulnerability, insignificance, or freedom, depending on the space. Wide shots create distance between the audience and the subject's interior life, favoring observation over intimacy. They can make a character feel exposed, watched, or lost.

## When to use
Use a wide shot to open a scene, to reveal a new location, to show blocking and movement, to emphasize scale, or to isolate a figure in their surroundings. Returning to a wide after a run of close-ups resets the space and can feel like emotional withdrawal.

## Variations
The extreme wide shot (EWS) renders the subject tiny or nearly lost in a vast landscape or cityscape, emphasizing scale and solitude. The establishing shot is a wide used specifically to set place and time at the start of a sequence.
