# Medium Shot (MS)

A medium shot typically frames a subject from roughly the waist up.

## Purpose
The medium shot is the workhorse of narrative coverage. It balances the subject's body language and gestures with enough of the environment to keep them grounded in the scene. It is close enough to read facial expression yet wide enough to show physical action and interaction between characters.

## Emotional effect
The medium shot is neutral and naturalistic — it approximates the distance of ordinary conversation. It keeps the audience engaged with the character without the pressure of a close-up. Two characters in a shared medium (a two-shot) shows their relationship, spacing, and body language toward each other.

## When to use
Use medium shots for dialogue, for physical business, for two-shots showing relationships, and as the connective tissue between wide establishing shots and emotional close-ups. It is the default from which a scene departs and returns.

## Variations
The medium-wide (or American shot) frames from the knees up, giving more body and movement. The medium close-up frames from mid-chest up, edging toward intimacy while retaining some body language — a common choice for pushing gently toward emotion.
