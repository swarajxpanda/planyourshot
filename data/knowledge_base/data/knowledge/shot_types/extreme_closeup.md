# Extreme Close-Up (ECU / XCU)

An extreme close-up isolates a single detail — a pair of eyes, a mouth, a trembling hand, an object, or a fragment of a face.

## Purpose
The extreme close-up magnifies a detail to maximum significance. On a face it isolates the eyes or mouth, abstracting the person into pure emotion or intent. On an object it forces the audience to register something the story hinges on — a message on a screen, a key, a wound, a trigger.

## Emotional effect
ECUs are intense and often uncomfortable. They can feel claustrophobic, invasive, or hyper-focused, collapsing the world down to one charged detail. Rapid cutting between ECUs fragments a moment into visceral pieces, common in tension, violence, or panic. A slow ECU on the eyes conveys deep interiority.

## When to use
Use an ECU to draw attention to a critical detail, to intensify a peak emotional beat, to build suspense by fixating on a small element (a phone lighting up, a doorknob turning), or to fragment a moment for visceral impact. Because it abstracts and isolates, it is a spice, not a staple.

## Variations
The insert ECU highlights a plot-critical object. The macro ECU on skin, an eye, or texture abstracts the image and can feel dreamlike or unsettling.
